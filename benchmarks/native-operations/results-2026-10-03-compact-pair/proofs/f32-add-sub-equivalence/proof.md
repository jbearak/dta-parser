# Compact addition and subtraction: equivalence argument and qualification

This argument has been checked by a standalone diagnostic on this host. No production addition or subtraction path has changed; integrated dispatch, captured ownership, package compiler behavior and performance remain separate qualification requirements.

Admission would remain limited to full-length, non-temporal compact byte/int16/binary32 pairs, at least one binary32 input, and an initially binary32 result. Byte and int16 convert exactly to binary32. Long, scalar binary64 arguments, ordinary doubles, and division are excluded. Existing source-format missing/NaN/infinity interpretation and captured-source ownership remain requirements.

For finite nonzero input magnitudes a and b, order them so |a| >= |b|, and let d be their normalized binary exponent difference. Each input can be expressed with an integer significand of at most 24 bits; subnormals can be normalized exactly by appending zero bits. Their exact sum or difference is an integer multiple of the smallest binary32 subnormal, so a nonzero result cannot underflow binary64. Its magnitude is less than 2^129, which cannot overflow binary64 or Stata's double observed range.

If d <= 28, alignment and a possible addition carry require at most 25 + d <= 53 significand bits. Cancellation reduces the needed precision. If d = 29, a carry above the aligned 53-bit significand is impossible: the larger significand leaves a gap of at least 2^29 below that upper power of two, while the smaller significand is at most 2^24 - 1. Thus binary64 computes the exact result for every d <= 29. Its subsequent binary32 rounding agrees with direct binary32 arithmetic.

If d >= 30, write e = floor(log2(|a|)). The smaller input is less than 2^(e-29), whereas the distance from a to the closest binary32 rounding midpoint is at least 2^(e-25). The latter bound includes the smaller spacing immediately below a binade boundary. The combined displacement is bounded by |b| plus the binary64 rounding error: less than 2^(e-29) + 2^(e-53) < 2^(e-25), still inside that midpoint margin. Round-to-nearest therefore returns a both directly and after binary64 evaluation. There can be no substantial cancellation in this case. Since the smaller input's exponent cannot be below -149, this large-gap case also implies e >= -119, so the larger input is normal.

For directed rounding, the finite binary32 grid is a subset of the binary64 grid. Rounding down twice through these nested grids equals rounding down directly; the corresponding statement holds for rounding up. Rounding toward zero applies the appropriate direction on each side of zero. The finite-domain bounds avoid binary64 underflow/overflow complications. The diagnostic explicitly tests exact zero signs: same-sign zeros, opposite-sign zeros, exact finite cancellation, and subtraction's exact negation of the second zero under all four modes.

The established contract uses the rounded binary64 result for storage decisions, even when that result differs from the exact rational sum. Assuming the arithmetic equivalence above, a rounded binary32 magnitude strictly below or above the exactly representable Stata limit again decides fit or promotion. Equality remains ambiguous and must execute the existing binary64 producer, followed by complete original-input recomputation if double storage is selected. This fallback must not be replaced by an inverse threshold or a rounded-result fit test. For example, under nearest rounding L + 1 is exactly above L but rounds to L in binary64; the current contract therefore retains float storage. Under upward rounding, the binary64 result advances above L and instead requires double storage. The oracle must reproduce that existing distinction rather than choosing storage directly from the exact rational sum.

The standalone oracle must test both operations independently and retain the source-extracted raw missing classifiers. It should cover normalized exponent gaps 28, 29, 30, and 31 explicitly; same/opposite signs; carry and cancellation; immediately adjacent values on both sides of powers of two; all subnormal boundaries; signed zeros; modern noncanonical high finite imports; every missing tag and neighboring raw encodings; and legacy layouts. It must verify all four rounding modes with operation-specific witnesses, compare result bits/storage/missing unions, and test late whole-column promotion with precision-sensitive earlier lanes. Exact rational checks should independently verify representative exponent-gap and limit witnesses, including a case where binary64 addition itself rounds a very small operand away. No performance claim follows from this argument.

## Qualification recorded on 2026-10-03

`run.py` extracted the current byte, int16 and float missing classifiers from committed `numeric-payload.c`, recorded their exact text and hashes, and compiled the standalone model with the recorded Clang binary. Both optimized and undefined-behavior-sanitized builds use `-O2 -ffp-contract=off -frounding-math`, explicit `STDC FENV_ACCESS`, `fesetround`/`fegetround`, and positive/negative operation-specific rounding witnesses. The diagnostic takes no performance timings.

Both builds passed 70,004,784 pair comparisons, 8,750,598 for each of two operations and four rounding modes, with zero result-bit, storage or missing mismatches and identical checksum `4aeef62749debc9b`. Each mode resets its boundary witnesses and records its own coverage. The 38 whole-column models check intersecting 3/4-row spans, late promotion, original-input precision after promotion, missing unions and discarded provisional counters.

The fixtures enumerate every physical byte/int16 code against selected raw float endpoints, all 27 modern missing codes and their immediate neighbors, both source layouts, both signs, NaN/infinity, zero/subnormal boundaries, large finite imports, deterministic raw random pairs, and systematic normalized exponent gaps 28/29/30/31/52/53/54/100. Modern high finite observations are not narrowed to constructor-produced values. Addition/subtraction produce invalid results from every observed infinity, matching this operator-specific existing contract. This claim does not apply to division.

`exact-witnesses.py` independently uses Python integers and Fractions, without host floating arithmetic, to implement binary32 and binary64 IEEE rounding. It passed 814 recorded witnesses across all eight operation/mode combinations. It checks direct binary32 and nested binary64-then-binary32 bits, signed zero/cancellation, exact and rounded storage limits, binade and exponent-gap edges, and strict promotion. In particular, it first rounds the exact rational expression to binary64 before selecting storage; some exact results lie above the Stata limit while the established rounded binary64 value still fits.
