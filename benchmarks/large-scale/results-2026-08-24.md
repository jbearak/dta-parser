# Rust 1.98 and dictionary-backed string results (2026-08-24)

Environment: Apple M4 Max, 128 GB RAM, macOS 26.5.2, R 4.6.1, haven 2.5.5,
and Stata/MP 18. The
pre-change baseline used Homebrew rustc 1.97.1; the final package used Homebrew
rustc 1.98.0 and the R bridge's optimized release profile. Both used the same
deterministic, warm-cache, 40-column Stata 15 files (DTA file-format release
119). Exact internal-collector
identity and sampled haven parity passed before timing.

## Release-119 multicore refresh

After the parallel decoder was extended across releases, dta-parser alone was
rerun on the same two synthetic files at commit `2d09478`, using its default
automatic thread count. Both files identify themselves as release 119 in their
DTA signatures; the generator writes them with
`haven::write_dta(version = 15)`. Each workload was warmed up and measured
seven times, and peak RSS was measured in three fresh processes. The
single-thread dta-parser, haven, and Stata cells were not rerun, so they retain
their archived values below.

| Input | Workload | Single-thread dta-parser | Multicore dta-parser | Reduction | Multicore dta-parser peak RSS | dta-parser / haven time | dta-parser / Stata time |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 100 MB | Full, 40 columns | 0.139 s | 0.073 s | 47.5% | 0.276 GB | 0.069x | 6.636x |
| 100 MB | Projected, 8 columns | 0.051 s | 0.035 s | 31.4% | 0.201 GB | 0.129x | 2.500x |
| 1 GB | Full, 40 columns | 0.911 s | 0.258 s | 71.7% | 0.839 GB | 0.024x | 2.554x |
| 1 GB | Projected, 8 columns | 0.333 s | 0.130 s | 61.0% | 0.302 GB | 0.042x | 0.929x |

The ratio columns are refreshed dta-parser time divided by archived comparator
time, the reciprocals of the previously reported ratios. Values below 1x favor
dta-parser. The 1 GB projected read took 0.929 times Stata's time; Stata remains
faster on the other three workloads, where dta-parser took 2.50--6.64 times as
long. The sections below preserve the original single-thread experiment and
its matched string-workload analysis as the implementation baseline.

The baseline was captured before edits with three measured iterations. The
final result used seven measured iterations, alternating implementation order
and running garbage collection outside timed regions. These are report-only
measurements, not CI thresholds.

| Input | Workload | Baseline dta-parser median | Final dta-parser median | Load-time reduction | Final p05--p95 | haven median | Stata median | dta-parser / haven time | dta-parser / Stata time |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 100 MB | Full, 40 columns | 0.307 s | 0.139 s | 54.7% | 0.139--0.189 s | 1.053 s | 0.011 s | 0.132x | 12.636x |
| 100 MB | Projected, 8 columns | 0.086 s | 0.051 s | 40.7% | 0.051--0.052 s | 0.271 s | 0.014 s | 0.188x | 3.643x |
| 1 GB | Full, 40 columns | 2.354 s | 0.911 s | 61.3% | 0.875--0.947 s | 10.958 s | 0.101 s | 0.083x | 9.020x |
| 1 GB | Projected, 8 columns | 0.821 s | 0.333 s | 59.4% | 0.305--0.370 s | 3.120 s | 0.140 s | 0.107x | 2.379x |

Both comparison ratios are final dta-parser time divided by comparator time;
values below 1x favor dta-parser and values above 1x favor the comparator.

This is an eager Stata comparison. Stata's official
[`use` documentation](https://www.stata.com/manuals/duse.pdf) specifies that the
command loads the DTA dataset into memory, and its
[User's Guide](https://www.stata.com/manuals/u.pdf) describes Stata as working
with a copy of data loaded into memory. The 1.133 GB peak RSS for the 1 GB full
load corroborates that behavior. The likely reasons for Stata's speed are its
native file and in-memory storage widths, its mature closed-source loader, and
the warm page cache—not deferred dataset loading. That explanation is an
inference; Stata does not publish the loader implementation.

The final 1 GB full-load median was 1.098 GB/s. Stata's time covers only its
`use` command, matching the R timings' exclusion of application startup. Its
projected `use varlist using` operation was slower than its full read on these
generated files; that measured result is retained as-is. The internal eager
Rust-vector collector's medians in that run were 2.205 s for the full load and
0.786 s for the projection.

## Matched string workloads

The original raw-string deferral prototype's 0.905 s load and 5.33 s
load-plus-`object.size()` measurements were not equivalent to the eager
baseline workload. The final implementation instead interns distinct strings
once per column, creates one R string per dictionary value, and defers only the
full row-level string-pointer vector. A snapshot of the old eager dta-parser
implementation was rebuilt with the same Rust 1.98 compiler and measured three
times; the final implementation was measured seven times. Each number includes
loading the same 1 GB file and performing the named consumer.

| Consumer | Eager median | Dictionary ALTREP median | Reduction |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Dimensions only | 2.026 s | 0.913 s | 54.9% |
| 1,024 distributed rows from every string column | 2.025 s | 0.909 s | 55.1% |
| Scan every numeric column | 2.194 s | 1.033 s | 52.9% |
| Scan every string | 2.075 s | 1.097 s | 47.1% |
| `object.size()` | 2.256 s | 1.722 s | 23.7% |

The hybrid therefore retains the partial-access benefit without the raw
prototype's forcing cliff. Even the every-string and `object.size()` workloads
remain faster than the eager implementation.

## Peak memory

Fresh processes loaded each synthetic dataset and retained the result until
exit. R values are medians of three runs; Stata values are medians of seven.
The dimensions-only R workload leaves dictionary vectors deferred. Peak RSS
includes R or Stata startup even though the reader timing above does not.

| Input | Workload | dta-parser peak RSS | haven peak RSS | Stata peak RSS |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 100 MB | Full, 40 columns | 0.273 GB | 0.253 GB | 0.143 GB |
| 100 MB | Projected, 8 columns | 0.198 GB | 0.168 GB | 0.058 GB |
| 1 GB | Full, 40 columns | 0.834 GB | 0.888 GB | 1.133 GB |
| 1 GB | Projected, 8 columns | 0.299 GB | 0.292 GB | 0.365 GB |

For the 1 GB full load, peak memory footprint was 0.817 GB for dta-parser,
0.872 GB for haven, and 1.120 GB for Stata. The following matched 1 GB table
adds the old eager collector and the R-specific `object.size()` forcing case.
The pre-change value is one eager run; final and haven values are medians of
three. `object.size()` deliberately forces the complete row-level string-pointer
vectors.

| Workload and measurement | Pre-change dta-parser | Final dta-parser | haven | Final vs pre-change | Final vs haven |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Load/dimensions, maximum RSS | 2.184 GB | 0.834 GB | 0.888 GB | 61.8% lower | 6.1% lower |
| Load/dimensions, peak footprint | 2.099 GB | 0.817 GB | 0.872 GB | 61.1% lower | 6.3% lower |
| Load + `object.size()`, maximum RSS | 2.184 GB | 0.975 GB | 1.313 GB | 55.3% lower | 25.7% lower |
| Load + `object.size()`, peak footprint | 2.099 GB | 0.936 GB | 1.297 GB | 55.4% lower | 27.8% lower |

Dictionary character columns own compact `u32` row indices and an R-protected
vector containing one normalized UTF-8 string per distinct value. They do not
retain or reopen the input file. Element access resolves an index directly;
operations requesting a contiguous R string-pointer vector materialize that
vector once and release the native indices.
