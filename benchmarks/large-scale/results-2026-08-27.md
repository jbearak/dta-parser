# Synthetic DTA writer results

Run date: 2026-08-27.

Environment: Apple M4 Max, 128 GB RAM, macOS 26.5.2, R 4.6.1,
dtaparser 0.5.0, Haven 2.5.5, and Stata/MP 18. The current package source was
built and installed into a private benchmark library before the run.

The suite measured dtaparser, Stata, and Haven writes seven times each on
deterministic approximately 100 MB and 1 GB inputs. Every writer ran in a fresh
process, and writer order rotated. The operation timer began after that
writer's own reader loaded the input. Peak RSS covers the whole process,
including the in-memory input; it is therefore a workflow memory measurement,
not an isolated writer-allocation measurement.

This historical fixture had 15 Stata `long` columns, 15 Stata `double`
columns, and 10 fixed-width string columns. Four longs carried value labels;
the double group included 11 ordinary numeric columns, two dates, and two
datetimes. It did not exercise Stata `byte`, `int`, or `float` storage.

| Input | Writer | Median time | 5th--95th percentile | Median peak RSS | Median output |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 100 MB | dtaparser | 2.825 s | 2.814--2.852 s | 0.386 GB | 100.000 MB |
| 100 MB | Stata | 0.012 s | 0.012--0.013 s | 0.155 GB | 100.000 MB |
| 100 MB | Haven | 0.474 s | 0.470--0.491 s | 0.255 GB | 113.359 MB |
| 1 GB | dtaparser | 27.034 s | 26.920--27.081 s | 1.704 GB | 1.000 GB |
| 1 GB | Stata | 0.116 s | 0.115--0.121 s | 1.145 GB | 1.000 GB |
| 1 GB | Haven | 4.310 s | 4.305--4.341 s | 0.909 GB | 1.134 GB |

On these inputs, dtaparser's median write time was 5.96 times Haven's at
100 MB and 6.27 times Haven's at 1 GB. Stata was much faster than both R
workflows. The measurement says where optimization work remains; it is not a
correctness gate or a cross-machine performance guarantee.

## Optimized dtaparser rerun

After commit `0fd0986` established the original writer checkpoint, the
dtaparser write path was optimized and measured again for seven iterations.
This rerun did not execute Stata or Haven. It verified the input hashes and
combined the new dtaparser rows with the unchanged reference rows above.

| Input | Writer | Measurement | Median time | 5th--95th percentile | Median peak RSS |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 100 MB | dtaparser | optimized rerun | 0.479 s | 0.476--0.481 s | 0.298 GB |
| 100 MB | Haven | fixed reference | 0.474 s | 0.470--0.491 s | 0.255 GB |
| 1 GB | dtaparser | optimized rerun | 3.826 s | 3.788--3.891 s | 0.894 GB |
| 1 GB | Haven | fixed reference | 4.310 s | 4.305--4.341 s | 0.909 GB |

Compared with the checkpoint, dtaparser's median time fell 83.0% at 100 MB
and 85.8% at 1 GB. Peak RSS fell 22.7% and 47.5%, respectively. The optimized
1 GB write was 11.2% faster than the fixed Haven median and used 1.7% less peak
RSS. The 100 MB write was 1.1% slower and used 16.8% more peak RSS, where fixed
process and preparation costs account for a larger share of the run.

Three changes produced the improvement: replacing per-element
`ISOdatetime()` reconstruction with UTC-offset arithmetic, borrowing protected
R string bytes in Rust instead of allocating a `String` for every cell, and
planning character and temporal conversions without materializing duplicate R
vectors.

## Redesigned write matrices

The write suite now separates two questions. Its primary matrix compares only
dtaparser and Stata on 4 `byte`, 4 `int`, 9 `long`, 4 `float`, 9 `double`, and
10 string columns. Four longs are value-labelled; two doubles are dates and two
are datetimes. In each iteration, Stata generates the complete data in memory
and times its first `save`. dtaparser then loads and saves that exact output.
The matching per-pair input SHA-256 values in the raw report enforce that
identity.

The historical Stata gap should not be read as a pure encoder comparison.
Stata saved already-native columns, while dtaparser's timed call validated the
R object, crossed its R/C/Rust value-source boundary for serialization, and
synchronized the completed file to storage. The old Stata worker also left the
loaded data unchanged, so the harness did not rule out an unchanged-save fast
path. The Stata-first-save run does:

| Scale | Writer | Median time | 5th--95th percentile | Median peak RSS | Median output |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 100 MB | dtaparser | 0.430 s | 0.427--0.434 s | 0.420 GB | 93.969 MB |
| 100 MB | Stata | 0.012 s | 0.012--0.013 s | 0.212 GB | 100.000 MB |
| 1 GB | dtaparser | 4.104 s | 4.084--4.134 s | 1.057 GB | 939.677 MB |
| 1 GB | Stata | 0.118 s | 0.116--0.120 s | 1.307 GB | 1.000 GB |

Stata's first-save medians are effectively the same as its earlier load/save
medians. The unchanged-save hypothesis is therefore false on this machine.
dtaparser took 35.8 times as long at 100 MB and 34.8 times as long at 1 GB,
despite writing about 6% fewer bytes after inferring narrower string widths.
The remaining gap lies in the write paths themselves, including dtaparser's
validation, per-value R/C/Rust calls, and durable synchronization.
